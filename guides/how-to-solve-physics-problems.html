How to Solve Physics Problems: Complete Step-by-Step Guide

April 12, 2026 • 14 min read • Guide

Physics problems can seem intimidating, but with a systematic approach, anyone can master them. This comprehensive guide teaches you how to solve physics problems step by step, covering force, motion, energy, electricity, and more. Learn the framework that successful physics students use.

The Problem-Solving Framework

Successful physics problem-solving follows a consistent framework. Master this framework and you can tackle any physics problem with confidence.

Step 1: Identify Given Information

Read the problem carefully and list all known quantities. Include numerical values, constants, and relationships. Convert all quantities to SI units (meters, kilograms, seconds) before calculating. This unit conversion step prevents most calculation errors.

Step 2: Determine the Unknown

Identify what you need to find. State the unknown clearly in your own words. Sometimes the unknown is implied rather than explicitly stated. Knowing exactly what you're solving for guides your choice of formulas and approach.

Step 3: Draw a Diagram

Create a visual representation. Show objects, forces with arrows, directions of motion, and label known quantities. A good diagram helps you visualize the situation and identify relationships between quantities. It also helps you catch information you might have missed.

Step 4: Choose Appropriate Principles

Select the physics principles that connect knowns to unknowns. Newton's laws for force problems, kinematic equations for motion, energy conservation for energy problems, Ohm's law for electricity. The right principle makes the problem straightforward.

Step 5: Apply Formulas Systematically

Write down relevant formulas. Substitute known values, showing all steps. Solve step by step, showing intermediate results. This systematic approach prevents errors and makes checking your work easier. Never skip steps in your written work.

Step 6: Check Units and Reasonableness

Verify your answer has correct units. Check if the magnitude makes sense. Use dimensional analysis and common sense. If you calculate a person walking at 50 m/s (over 100 mph), something's wrong. Catch errors before submitting your answer.

Step 7: Verify with Simulation

Use interactive PhET simulations to verify calculations. After solving a projectile motion problem, use the Projectile Motion simulation to check your predicted trajectory. Visual verification builds confidence and deepens understanding.

Common Physics Formulas

Essential Formulas

  • Newton's Second Law: F = ma
  • Kinetic Energy: KE = ½mv²
  • Potential Energy: PE = mgh
  • Work: W = Fd cos(θ)
  • Ohm's Law: V = IR
  • Power: P = VI = I²R
