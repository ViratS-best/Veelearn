How to Use PhET Simulations: Complete Step-by-Step Guide

April 12, 2026 • 12 min read • Guide

PhET interactive simulations are powerful tools for learning science and mathematics through hands-on experimentation. This comprehensive guide will teach you how to effectively use PhET simulations to build conceptual understanding, test predictions, and deepen your knowledge of physics, chemistry, math, and biology.

What Are PhET Simulations?

PhET Interactive Simulations, developed at the University of Colorado Boulder, are research-based interactive simulations that make abstract STEM concepts visible and manipulable. These simulations allow students to explore phenomena by manipulating variables, observing cause-and-effect relationships, and conducting virtual experiments in a safe, repeatable environment.

Research shows that students who use PhET simulations develop 40% deeper conceptual understanding than those who rely only on textbooks and lectures. The visual, interactive approach helps build accurate mental models of scientific phenomena.

Step-by-Step Guide

Step 1: Choose the Right Simulation

Browse the PhET simulation library and select a simulation that matches your learning objectives. Simulations are organized by subject:

  • Physics: Forces and Motion, Energy Skate Park, Circuit Construction Kit, Wave Interference
  • Chemistry: Reactants, Products, and Leftovers, pH Scale, Balancing Chemical Equations
  • Math: Graphing Lines, Function Builder, Curve Fitting, Arithmetic
  • Biology: Natural Selection, Gene Expression Essentials, Membrane Channels

Step 2: Launch the Simulation

Click on the simulation to launch it in your browser. Most PhET simulations run directly in the web browser without requiring any downloads. Ensure you have a modern web browser like Chrome, Firefox, or Safari for best performance. Some simulations may take a moment to load initially.

Step 3: Explore the Controls

Familiarize yourself with the simulation controls before starting:

  • Sliders: Adjust variables like mass, velocity, temperature, or concentration
  • Buttons: Play, pause, reset, and clear functions
  • Draggable elements: Move objects, resize items, or place components
  • Measurement tools: Rulers, thermometers, voltmeters, and other instruments

Step 4: Run the Simulation

Press the play button to start the simulation. Watch how the system responds to the initial conditions. Pause the simulation at any time to observe the state and take notes. Use the slow-motion feature if available to observe fast processes in detail.

Step 5: Modify Variables

Use sliders and controls to change variables. Observe how changing each variable affects the system. For example, in Forces and Motion, change the applied force and observe how acceleration changes. In Energy Skate Park, change the skater's mass and see how kinetic and potential energy change.

Step 6: Record Observations

Take notes on what you observe. Many simulations include data tables, graphs, and measurement tools. Use these to collect quantitative data. Record both qualitative observations and quantitative measurements for later analysis.

Step 7: Test Predictions

Before changing a variable, predict what will happen based on your understanding of the concepts. Then make the change and compare the result with your prediction. This active prediction-testing process deepens understanding more than passive observation.

Step 8: Connect to Theory

Relate your observations to the theoretical concepts you're learning. Identify which physical laws, chemical principles, or mathematical relationships the simulation demonstrates. This connection between theory and practice reinforces learning and helps you remember concepts longer.

Tips for Effective Learning

  • Start with simple scenarios before exploring complex ones
  • Use the reset button frequently to try different approaches
  • Work with a partner and discuss your observations
  • Connect simulation results to homework problems
  • Take screenshots of interesting configurations for reference
  • Use the simulation to verify your calculations
