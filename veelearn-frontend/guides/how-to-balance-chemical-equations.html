How to Balance Chemical Equations: Complete Step-by-Step Guide

April 12, 2026 • 13 min read • Guide

Balancing chemical equations is a fundamental skill in chemistry that ensures the Law of Conservation of Mass is obeyed. This comprehensive guide teaches you how to balance chemical equations step by step, from simple synthesis reactions to complex redox reactions.

The Law of Conservation of Mass

Chemical equations must be balanced because matter cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction. The number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides of the equation. Balancing ensures the equation accurately represents the actual chemical transformation.

Step-by-Step Method

Step 1: Write the Unbalanced Equation

Start with correct chemical formulas for all reactants and products. Never change subscripts—subscripts define the compound's identity. Changing H₂O to H₂O₂ creates hydrogen peroxide instead of water.

Step 2: Count Atoms on Each Side

Create a table showing atom counts. This reveals which elements are unbalanced. Count carefully for elements appearing in multiple compounds.

Step 3: Balance Elements Appearing Once

Start with elements appearing in only one compound on each side. These are easiest to balance. Save oxygen and hydrogen for last.

Step 4: Use Coefficients, Never Subscripts

Place coefficients in front of formulas to balance. Never change subscripts. Coefficients multiply the entire formula.

Step 5: Balance Polyatomic Ions as Units

When polyatomic ions remain intact on both sides, balance them as complete units. This simplifies the process.

Step 6: Balance Oxygen and Hydrogen Last

Oxygen and hydrogen appear in multiple compounds, making them trickier. Balance them after other elements.

Step 7: Verify and Reduce

Count all atoms to verify balance. If coefficients share a common factor, divide to express in smallest whole number ratio.
