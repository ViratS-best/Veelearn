Lesson 12: The Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation

To understand the evolution of a quantum system over time, we must look beyond the stationary states. The Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation (TDSE) provides the fundamental framework for determining how the wave function changes.

JV

Dr. Julian Vance

Professor of Theoretical Physics

Understanding the Foundation

The Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation is fundamental to quantum mechanics. The wave function $\Psi(\mathbf{r}, t)$ describes the probability amplitude for finding a particle at position $\mathbf{r}$ at time $t$.

$$i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \Psi(\mathbf{r}, t) = \left[ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \nabla^2 + V(\mathbf{r}, t) \right] \Psi(\mathbf{r}, t)$$

Where $i$ is the imaginary unit, $\hbar$ is the reduced Planck constant, and the term in brackets represents the Hamiltonian operator $\hat{H}(t)$.

Physical Interpretation

Unlike classical mechanics, where particles follow definite trajectories determined by Newton's laws, quantum mechanics describes the probability density:

$$P(\mathbf{r}, t) = |\Psi(\mathbf{r}, t)|^2$$
Note for Students: The Hamiltonian operator above represents the total energy of the system, including kinetic and potential energies.
